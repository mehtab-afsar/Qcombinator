The one-line version

We built a good engine and connected it to almost nothing.

58⁄61Actions end in prose
Our executives write excellent memos about work instead of doing it. This document is about attaching the hands — and about the two architectural holes that must close first, or every integration we add becomes a disconnected island.

The two root causes

Everything else in this document follows from these.

1 · The Connector contract is email-shaped

ConnectorRequest = { idempotencyKey, recipients[], subject, body, channel? }

That is the entire vocabulary a connector has. There is no way to express "find companies matching this ICP," "get a verified email for this person," "book a 30-minute call," or "publish this page."

Consequence: a connector that is not a messaging connector cannot currently exist.

2 · There is no entity layer

founder_contacts is the only real-world business entity in the system. It is standalone — its single foreign key points at the founder, and it links to no Program, Action, or Asset. It is written by the founder in the UI and never by an Action. It is consumed as prose, rendered into a prompt as text.

Meanwhile the structured JSON an Action produces is parsed, briefly vaulted, read for four email fields, and then deleted. Action-to-action chaining passes paragraphs between prompts, not records.

Consequence: there is no table an Action can write a record into. Nothing accumulates.

These two holes are the founder's complaint

“Who gives a damn about position — I can say go mail the CEO of that company. But I need to get the email of that guy, that's more important.”

→ Root cause 1. The pipeline can reason about who to contact but has no capability to find them, because “find a person” is inexpressible in the connector contract.

“The AI SDR will say CEO or CTO of so-and-so company, but my contact list has the number of my barber. There is a mismatch.”

→ Root cause 2. Research output is prose inside a document; contacts are a disconnected hand-typed table. They can never meet, because nothing an Action produces can become a contact.

The AI SDR isn't badly built. It is a well-built pipeline with no hands and no memory.

Part 3

The spine

The ask is for leaves: lead gen, LinkedIn, booking, scheduling, publishing. The honest engineering answer is that leaves without a spine produce twenty disconnected integrations and the exact same mismatch problem, at twenty times the cost. Three primitives, in dependency order.

A capability contract

Generalise ConnectorRequest from “a message” into a typed capability invocation. Keep send/reconcile/revoke, the grants table, the vault handling, and the generic OAuth routes exactly as they are — all of that is good, and none of it needs to change. Six classes, which is also the map of everything we'd ever build:

ClassDirectionExamples
Finddata in, on demandlead search, email enrichment, company data, web research
Reachcomms outemail, LinkedIn (drafted), SMS, WhatsApp
Schedulebothcalendar, booking links, reminders
Publishoutwebsite, blog, social, docs
Transactoutinvoices, payments, contracts, e-sign
Observedata in, continuousanalytics, inbox, CRM, bank, accounting

An entity layer

Real records that Actions read and write, that the founder can see and edit: leads, meetings, candidates, invoices, investors.

The critical link: a researched lead, once enriched with a verified email, becomes a contact. That single reconciliation closes the mismatch. “CEO of Acme” stops being a sentence in a document and becomes a row with a status, a history, and a next action.

Structured action results

The typed JSON the model already emits must survive into entities instead of being discarded. Chained steps should pass records, not paragraphs.

Everything else hangs off these three. Build leaves first and we build the spine twice.

Part 5

Hard truths

Researched, not assumed. These change the design.

Do not build

LinkedIn automation

There is no sanctioned path. The official partner API cannot send connection requests, cannot DM, and cannot scrape. None of the consumer outreach tools are approved partners. 2026 enforcement escalated sharply — sessions flagged within 48 hours, suspensions rather than warnings — and in March 2026 HeyReach's own company page and founder profile were banned.

Recommendation: draft-and-queue only. The AI decides who and writes what; the founder clicks send inside LinkedIn. Most of the value, zero ban risk. Anything headless risks the founder's account, and potentially ours.

Design constraint

Cold email will burn the domain if done naively

The practical 2026 ceiling is 20–50 sends per mailbox per day, not Gmail's technical 2,000. DMARC p=reject is the effective default at Gmail and Outlook, the spam-complaint ceiling is 0.10%, one-click unsubscribe is mandatory, and unverified lists now actively trip bounce-rate compliance gates.

Never send SDR volume from the founder's primary inbox. This needs a dedicated sending subdomain, a warmup ramp, hard daily caps, and verification before every send — designed in from the start, not bolted on.

Needs a decision

ADR-004 is a wall, and it is load-bearing

Every irreversible act requires per-payload, just-in-time approval, and ADR-020 deliberately closes the recurring-action loophole. Fifty outreach emails means fifty approvals. That is unusable.

Proposed amendment: batch-payload approval. The founder reviews N prepared payloads and approves once, with the hash binding that exact set — change any byte and the approval is void. This preserves ADR-004's actual principle (the founder saw the exact content before it left) while making volume viable. ADR-004's reasoning is sound and its rejection of unattended irreversible actions should survive intact.

Positioning

The category has a credibility problem, not a capability gap

11x was publicly reported claiming customers it did not have, and ZoomInfo said it “performed significantly worse than our SDR employees.” Artisan has documented reviews calling output “obviously AI,” including one campaign of 1,400 emails with zero responses.

Being another AI SDR is not a differentiator. Ours operates under a mandate, derived from a Q-Score, with outcomes that feed back. Apollo does not know your strategy. Calendly does not know your runway. That coherence is the moat — not the integrations themselves.

Reframe

The sequencing tension, stated honestly

Our own follow-ups doc and PRD §13.7 both pre-label connector breadth as the named scope-creep risk, gated behind the week-4 retention gate.

Counter-hypothesis worth taking seriously: documents nobody acts on may be the retention problem. If so, this work is what passes the gate, not what waits behind it. That reframing deserves a deliberate decision rather than a default.

Part 8

Three decisions needed before building

These are the founder's to make. This document deliberately does not pre-empt them.

Amend ADR-004 to allow batch-payload approval?

Without it, volume outbound is impossible. With it, we knowingly relax a locked safety decision — in spirit-preserving fashion, but knowingly.

Bring-your-own-account, or managed keys?

Recommendation is BYOK. It's a business-model decision, not a technical one.

Spine-first, or a fast Apollo win first?

Spine-first is correct and slower. A quick Apollo integration would demo well next week and be rebuilt within a quarter. Both are defensible — they should be chosen, not drifted into.
